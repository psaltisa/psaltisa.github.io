## Thanassis Psaltis <small>(he/him)</small>

<div class="profile float-left">
    <img class="img-fluid rounded-circle" src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/thanassis.JPG" />
</div>

Thanassis is an Assistant Professor of Physics at the Department of Astronomy and Physics at [Saint Mary's University](https://www.smu.ca/astronomy-physics/index.html) in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada.

He works in [nuclear astrophysics]({{ site.baseurl }}/research), an interdisciplinary field which studies the intricate relationship between stellar evolution and the creation of chemical elements in the universe.

Apart from his research, Thanassis enjoys long-distance running, playing chess and reading books. He also participates in various [science outreach]({{ site.baseurl }}/outreach) activities and channels his passion for science, philosophy, art, and other topics through his [personal blog]({{ site.baseurl }}/blog).

          <p class="card-text">
            We are seeking a motivated M.Sc. student to join the nuclear astrophysics group at Saint Mary's University, starting in September 2027.
          </p>

          <p class="card-text">
           Our group investigates how nuclear reactions shape the production of elements in stars and stellar explosions. We combine experiments with stable and radioactive ion beams, nuclear reaction modelling, computational nucleosynthesis and astronomical observations to understand environments such as supernovae and classical novae.
          </p>

          <h6 class="card-title mt-4">Research opportunities</h6>

          <p class="card-text">
            The successful candidate will be able to contribute to projects in several areas, including:
          </p>

          <ul class="card-text">
            <li><strong>Experimental nuclear astrophysics:</strong> Connecting nucleosynthesis models to measurements of astrophysically important nuclear reactions, including experiments at facilities such as TRIUMF (Canada), Argonne National Laboratory (USA), and RIKEN (Japan).</li>
            <li><strong>Nuclear reaction rates and uncertainties:</strong> Studying how uncertainties in nuclear properties affect astrophysical nucleosynthesis predictions and identifying priorities for future experimental campaigns.</li>
            <li><strong>Nucleosynthesis in stellar explosions:</strong> Modelling the production of heavy elements in supernovae and neutrino-driven outflows, and investigating the origin of abundance patterns observed in metal-poor stars.</li>
            <li><strong>Presolar grains and stellar nucleosynthesis:</strong> Using isotopic signatures preserved in stardust grains to investigate the conditions and nuclear reactions responsible for element production.</li>
          </ul>

          <p class="card-text">
            Projects will be developed in consultation with the successful candidate, taking into account their interests and background.
          </p>

          <h6 class="card-title mt-4">What we are looking for</h6>

          <p class="card-text">
            Applicants should have, or be completing, a B.Sc. in Physics, Astrophysics or a related discipline, and meet the admission requirements for the M.Sc. programs in Astronomy or Applied Science at Saint Mary's University (minimum GPA of 3.00/4.3). 
          </p>

          <p class="card-text">
            Experience with scientific programming or computational physics is an asset, but previous experience in nuclear astrophysics is not required.
            We are particularly interested in students who enjoy combining different areas of physics, are curious about the connection between nuclear reactions and astronomical observations and are motivated to develop their computational and research skills.
          </p>

          <p class="card-text">

## Shane Gervais
<div class="profile float-left">
    <img class="img-fluid rounded-circle" src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/img/group/Shane.jpeg" />
</div>
Shane is a graduate research student working with Drs. Greg Christian and Thanassis Psaltis. He is particularly interested in thermonuclear reaction rates in nova nucleosynthesis. Shane's research project involves creating a sensitivity study to address uncertainties in reaction rates, offering insight into key reaction rates that affect nova nucleosynthesis and those that would need further experimental investigation. He looks forward to collaborating with fellow researchers who are interested in the origin of the elements and fundamental particles of our universe.

## Spencer Reyno <small>(he/him)</small>
<div class="profile float-left">

Spencer is a fourth-year student at Saint Mary's University pursuing his honours in astrophysics, with a minor in mathematics. His research focus lies in the nuclear fusion reactions present in the thermonuclear runaway phase of classical novae, which are responsible for the genesis of various elements on the periodic table. In his free time, Spencer enjoys learning about operating systems, reading, watching, and writing fantasy media, as well as playing various kinds of video games. For as long as he can remember, Spencer has been passionate about physics and fantasy alike, and is more than happy to discuss either of these subjects with any who share these interests.
